# Product

## Register

product

## Users

ScaleCut is for video editors, content creators, agencies, marketing teams, and post-production operators who need to turn raw creative work into organized, platform-ready deliverables. They are usually working under delivery pressure, managing many clip versions, formats, naming rules, and project folders at once.

## Product Purpose

ScaleCut helps teams structure scalable video projects: project folders, naming conventions, delivery checklists, edit plans, status tracking, and future AI-assisted workflows for raw clip analysis, transcription, subtitles, branding, and version generation. Success means users can start, track, and package a multi-platform video project without manual naming chaos or forgotten deliverables.

## Brand Personality

Precise, creative, calm. The product should feel premium and modern, but it serves the workflow first. It should give creators confidence that every clip, format, asset, and status is under control.

## Anti-references

Avoid Adobe-like branding, red-first visual systems, generic AI-purple dashboards, noisy decorative glassmorphism, oversized marketing hero layouts, and interfaces that feel like a landing page instead of a working creative tool. Avoid playful emoji-heavy UI as the product matures; icons and labels should become more intentional.

## Design Principles

1. The workspace comes first: every screen should help the user move a video project forward.
2. Make scale visible: show project status, deliverable counts, formats, and next actions without forcing users to dig.
3. Use premium restraint: polished motion, contrast, and surfaces should support focus rather than decorate the page.
4. Build toward AI collaboration: design patterns should leave room for transcription, subtitles, branding, scene building, asset libraries, and prompt-based creative tools.
5. Keep creator trust: generated files, naming logic, and status changes should feel explicit, reversible, and easy to verify.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for body text and controls. Keep keyboard focus visible, preserve readable labels, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Motion should communicate feedback or state, never block repeated workflow actions.
